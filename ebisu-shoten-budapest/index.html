Ebisu is the only one of Japan's seven lucky gods said to be purely native — a fisherman's god, always smiling, always a little late, because he can't hear the other gods calling from the ceiling of heaven. He waits. Good things come to the patient.

Ebisu Shoten — "Ebisu's shop" — opened on a quiet street off Budapest's inner ring with the same idea. Sit at the counter. Let the coals do the work. Skewers over binchōtan charcoal, small plates built around what came in that morning, and a back bar stocked deep with sake, shōchū and Japanese whisky.

Distilled to
a short list.

Budapest is a city of contrasts too — grand boulevards and back-street bars, ruin pubs and quiet counters. Ebisu Shoten sits in that second Budapest: unmarked door, low light, no rush.

It's in the chicken turned by hand over binchōtan. It's in the thirty-odd bottles of sake kept at the right temperature, each one poured by someone who can tell you why. It's in keeping the menu short enough that everything on it is done properly.
